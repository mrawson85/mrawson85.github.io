Workstation Ergonomics

Root cause identified: Your secondary monitor is positioned both to the side and higher than the primary — forcing simultaneous head rotation and extension. This is the exact chronic loading pattern that overloads the levator scapulae. Fixing the physical environment stops the daily re-injury before any stretching can be effective.

Source: OSHA Computer Workstations eTool; Mayo Clinic Office Ergonomics Guide
Target setup: both monitors at eye level, keyboard at elbow height, 90° at hips and knees — Mayo Clinic / OSHA
🚨

Priority Fix — Secondary Monitor

  • Raise it to exactly match the primary monitor height
  • Use a monitor arm, riser, or solid stand
  • Keep it no more than 30–35° to the side
  • This one change outweighs all stretches combined
🖥️

Primary Monitor

  • Centred directly in front of you
  • Top of screen at or just below eye level
  • Natural gaze 10–15° downward
  • Arm's length distance (50–70 cm)
🪑

Chair & Keyboard Height

  • Elbows at ~90°, forearms parallel to floor
  • Wrists at or below elbow height — never above
  • Feet flat on floor or footrest
  • Lower back supported by chair or lumbar cushion
📐

Head & Neck

  • Ears stacked over shoulders at all times
  • No chin jutting forward (tech neck)
  • Never cradle a phone between ear and shoulder
  • Current glasses prescription — squinting drives neck extension
45 min

Maximum seated time before standing up and doing 2–3 shoulder rolls

20 min

Every 20 min: look away for 20 seconds and consciously drop your shoulders

Calls

Take phone and video calls standing — the easiest movement break available

Exercises & Stretches

Consistent daily practice at moderate intensity is far more effective than occasional aggressive stretching. These four exercises are the standard protocol recommended by physiatrists and physiotherapists for levator scapulae and upper trapezius overload.
⭐ Priority #1
Image: Spine-Health.com / Veritas Health — peer-reviewed by Dr. Zinovy Meyler, DO
Exercise 01

Levator Scapulae Stretch

Levator Scapulae

The most direct treatment for your symptoms. Targets the full length of the muscle from shoulder blade to upper cervical spine (C1–C4).

  1. 1Sit or stand tall. Actively press the shoulder down on the side being stretched.
  2. 2Tilt your head forward and toward the opposite side — ear toward opposite armpit, not directly sideways.
  3. 3Rest the opposite hand gently on the back of your head for a light assist. Do not force.
  4. 4Feel the pull between your neck and the top of the shoulder blade. Breathe slowly throughout.
  5. 5Hold 30–60 seconds. Release slowly. Switch sides.
Dose: 3 reps per side, 2–3× daily. The tighter side (secondary monitor side) gets an extra set.
Source: Meyler Z. Easy Levator Scapulae Stretch for Neck Pain. Spine-Health / Veritas Health. Peer-reviewed 2018. | Physiopedia: Levator Scapulae Syndrome.
Image: Spine-Health.com / Veritas Health
Exercise 02

Chin Tuck

Deep Cervical Flexors

Counteracts forward-head posture — the postural pattern that keeps the levator scapulae under chronic load. Think "tall neck," not "pull chin in."

  1. 1Sit tall, eyes level with the horizon.
  2. 2Place one finger lightly on your chin as a reference point.
  3. 3Glide your head straight backward — like a drawer sliding in. Gaze stays horizontal.
  4. 4Do NOT tilt the chin down. Movement is purely horizontal.
  5. 5Hold 5 seconds. Release. Repeat 10 times.
Dose: 10 reps, 5–7× throughout the day. Easy to do during calls or between tasks.
Source: Meyler Z. Easy Chin Tucks for Neck Pain. Spine-Health / Veritas Health. | Ahn S-H et al. (2020). Effect of Chin Tuck Exercises on Various Postures and Muscle Activity. PNF and Movement, 18(3), 403–414. | Kim J-H et al. (2018). Biomechanical Analysis of Chin Tuck Exercise. Int J Precision Engineering, 19(4).
Image: Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA)
Exercise 03

Shoulder Rolls — Back & Down

Upper Trapezius

Releases the upper trapezius and re-educates the shoulder's resting position. Directly counters the shoulder-hike caused by your current keyboard height.

  1. 1Sit or stand tall, arms relaxed at sides.
  2. 2Roll shoulders up, back, then DOWN in a slow deliberate circle. Emphasise the "down."
  3. 310 slow circles backward, then 10 forward.
  4. 4Finish: hold shoulders pressed down for 10 seconds.
Dose: Every hour, or immediately when tension starts building.
Source: Physiopedia: Trapezius — "Stretches can be done in repetitions of 15–20 every hour to decrease trapezius muscle pain." | Castelein B et al. (2016). Chronic neck pain, scapular dyskinesis and altered scapulothoracic muscle activity. J Electromyogr Kinesiol, 31:136–143.
Image: Spine-Health.com / Veritas Health — peer-reviewed by Dr. Zinovy Meyler, DO
Exercise 04

Corner / Doorway Chest Stretch

Pectoralis Major & Minor

Opens the tight chest muscles that pull the shoulders forward and load the posterior neck. Cumha et al. (2008) found stretching pectorals alongside the levator scapulae significantly improved cervical range of motion.

  1. 1Face a corner, feet together about 60 cm away.
  2. 2Place forearms on each wall, elbows at or slightly below shoulder height.
  3. 3Lean body weight forward until you feel a stretch across the chest and front of shoulders.
  4. 4Keep core slightly engaged — do not let the lower back arch.
  5. 5Hold 30–60 seconds.
Dose: Morning, mid-afternoon, before bed. 3–5× daily if tolerated.
Source: Meyler Z. Easy Chest Stretches for Neck Pain. Spine-Health / Veritas Health. | Cunha ACV et al. (2008). Effect of Global Posture Reeducation and Static Stretching on Pain and Range of Motion in Women with Chronic Neck Pain. Clinics (São Paulo), 63(6):763–770.

Heat & Self-Release

🔥

Heat Before Stretching

Apply a warm heat pack to the neck/upper shoulder for 10 minutes before stretching. Heat relaxes muscle fibres and significantly increases stretch effectiveness. Moist heat penetrates better than dry.

👋

Self-Massage

Using your opposite hand, apply firm circular pressure into the neck/shoulder junction for 1–2 minutes per side before stretching. Slow pressure on trigger points releases accumulated tension.

🎾

Tennis Ball Trigger Point Release

Place a tennis ball between your upper back/shoulder blade and a wall. Lean in to find tender spots. Hold on each spot 30–60 seconds. Move slowly — this is targeted pressure, not rolling. Effective for releasing levator scapulae trigger points that don't respond to stretching alone.

Supportive Supplements

These supplements do not replace ergonomic corrections or exercise, but have peer-reviewed evidence for reducing muscle tension, inflammation, and pain perception. Dosages are standard adult ranges — check with a GP or pharmacist if on any medications.
Supplement Why It Helps Dose Evidence
Magnesium Glycinate
 Muscle relaxant. Deficiency is strongly linked to muscle tension, spasm, and poor sleep. Glycinate form is best absorbed and gentlest on the stomach. 300–400 mg before bed
Gröber U et al. (2015). Magnesium in Prevention and Therapy. Nutrients, 7(9):8199–8226. PubMed
Omega-3 Fish Oil
 Anti-inflammatory. Reduces systemic inflammation in muscle and connective tissue. 4–6 weeks of consistent use for meaningful effect. 2–3 g EPA+DHA daily
Goldberg RJ, Katz J. (2007). A meta-analysis of the analgesic effects of omega-3 fatty acids. Pain, 129(1–2):210–23. PubMed
Curcumin + Piperine
 Anti-inflammatory via NF-kB pathway. Piperine (black pepper extract) increases absorption by up to 2,000%. Comparable to ibuprofen in some chronic pain trials. 500 mg + piperine, 2× daily
Daily JW et al. (2016). Efficacy of Turmeric Extracts and Curcumin for Alleviating Pain. J Med Food, 19(8):717–29. PubMed
Vitamin D3 + K2
 Deficiency independently associated with musculoskeletal pain. Northern latitude deficiency is extremely common, especially in winter months. 2,000–4,000 IU D3 daily
Straube S et al. (2015). Vitamin D for the treatment of chronic painful conditions in adults. Cochrane Database. PubMed
Collagen Peptides (Type I/III)
 Supports tendon and connective tissue repair. The levator scapulae attaches via tendons — collagen maintains elasticity and supports recovery from chronic overload. 10 g daily with vitamin C
Shaw G et al. (2017). Vitamin C–enriched gelatin supplementation before exercise increases collagen synthesis. AJCN, 105(1):136–143. PubMed
Vitamin C
 Required cofactor for collagen synthesis. Also an antioxidant reducing oxidative stress in overloaded tissue. Take alongside collagen for maximum benefit. 500–1,000 mg daily
DePhillipo NN et al. (2018). Efficacy of Vitamin C Supplementation on Collagen Synthesis. Orthopaedic J Sports Med, 6(10). PubMed

Topical Options

🧴 Magnesium Oil Spray

Applied directly to the tight neck/shoulder. Spray 5–10 pumps, massage gently in. Many report meaningful local muscle relaxation.

🌿 Arnica Gel

Well-tolerated topical anti-inflammatory. Apply to the upper shoulder/neck junction 2–3× daily. Available at pharmacies without prescription.

💊 Voltaren (Diclofenac Gel)

OTC NSAID gel. Reduces local inflammation without GI side effects of oral NSAIDs. Use for acute flare-ups of 5–7 days.

⚠️

When to See a Professional

If symptoms don't improve meaningfully within 4 weeks, or if pain radiates into the arm or causes tingling/numbness in the fingers, consult a physiotherapist or GP. This protocol addresses muscle-origin pain. Nerve involvement (radiculopathy) requires different management and should not be self-treated.
