Workstation Ergonomics

Root cause identified: Your secondary monitor is positioned both to the side and higher than the primary. This forces the head into simultaneous rotation and extension — the exact loading pattern that chronically overloads the levator scapulae. Fixing the environment eliminates the daily re-injury cycle and is the highest-leverage intervention available.
🚨

Priority Fix — Secondary Monitor Height

  • Raise it to exactly match the height of your primary monitor
  • Use a monitor arm, riser stand, or stack of solid books
  • Position it no more than 30–35° to the side
  • This single change will do more than all stretches combined
🖥️

Primary Monitor Position

  • Centred directly in front of you
  • Top of screen at or just below eye level
  • Natural gaze falls 10–15° downward
  • Arm's length distance (roughly 50–70 cm)
🪑

Chair & Keyboard Height

  • Raise chair until elbows rest at ~90° with forearms parallel to floor
  • Wrists at or below elbow height on keyboard — never above
  • Feet flat on floor or on footrest
  • Lower back supported by chair back or lumbar cushion
📐

Head & Neck Alignment

  • Ears stacked directly over shoulders
  • No chin jutting forward ("tech neck")
  • Never cradle a phone between ear and shoulder
  • Ensure current glasses prescription — squinting drives neck extension
45 min

Maximum seated time before standing, walking briefly, and doing 2–3 shoulder rolls

20 min

Every 20 minutes: look away from screen for 20 seconds and consciously drop your shoulders

Calls

Take phone and video calls standing when possible — the easiest movement break you can build in

Exercises & Stretches

These target the levator scapulae specifically, with supporting work for the upper trapezius and suboccipitals. Do them consistently at low intensity — occasional aggressive stretching is far less effective than daily gentle practice.
⭐ Priority #1
Exercise 01

Levator Scapulae Stretch

Levator Scapulae

The most direct treatment for your symptoms. Targets the full length of the muscle from shoulder blade to upper cervical spine.

  1. 1Sit or stand tall. Actively press the shoulder down on the side being stretched.
  2. 2Tilt your head forward and toward the opposite side — ear toward opposite armpit, not directly sideways.
  3. 3Place the opposite hand gently on the back of your head to add a light assist. No forcing.
  4. 4Feel the distinct pull between your neck and the top of your shoulder blade. Breathe slowly.
  5. 5Hold 30 seconds. Release slowly. Repeat on other side.
Dose: 3 reps per side, 2–3× daily. The tighter side (secondary monitor side) gets an extra set.
Exercise 02

Chin Tuck

Deep Cervical Flexors

Counteracts forward-head posture — the postural pattern that keeps the levator scapulae under constant load. Think "tall neck," not "pull chin in."

  1. 1Sit tall, eyes level with the horizon.
  2. 2Place one finger lightly on your chin as a reference point.
  3. 3Glide your head straight backward — like a drawer sliding in. Your gaze stays perfectly horizontal.
  4. 4Do NOT tilt the chin down. Movement is purely horizontal.
  5. 5Hold 5 seconds. Release. Repeat 10 times.
Dose: 10 reps every hour at your desk. Easy to do during a call or between tasks.
Exercise 03

Shoulder Rolls — Back & Down

Upper Trapezius

Releases the upper trapezius and re-educates the shoulder's resting position. Specifically counters the shoulder-hike caused by your current keyboard height.

  1. 1Sit or stand tall, arms relaxed at sides.
  2. 2Roll shoulders up, then back, then DOWN in a slow circle. Emphasise the "down" phase.
  3. 3Complete 10 slow circles backward, then 10 forward.
  4. 4Finish with a 10-second hold: shoulders pressed consciously downward.
Dose: Every hour at minimum, or immediately when you notice tension creeping in.
Exercise 04

Corner / Doorway Chest Stretch

Pectoralis Major & Minor

Opens tight chest muscles that contribute to the rounded-shoulder posture loading the posterior neck. Essential companion to the levator stretch.

  1. 1Face a corner or doorway, feet together about 60 cm away.
  2. 2Place forearms on each wall, elbows at or slightly below shoulder height.
  3. 3Lean body weight gently forward until you feel a stretch across the chest and front of shoulders.
  4. 4Keep core slightly engaged so your lower back doesn't arch.
  5. 5Hold 30–60 seconds.
Dose: Morning, mid-afternoon, and before bed. 3–5× daily if tolerated.

Heat & Self-Release

🔥

Heat Before Stretching

Apply a warm heat pack to the neck and upper shoulder for 10 minutes before doing the stretches above. Heat relaxes muscle fibres and significantly increases stretch effectiveness.

👋

Self-Massage

Using your opposite hand, apply firm circular pressure into the junction between your neck and top of shoulder. Work slowly for 1–2 minutes per side before stretching.

🎾

Tennis Ball Against Wall

For deeper trigger point release: place a tennis ball between your upper back/shoulder blade area and a wall. Lean into it to find tender spots and hold on each for 30–60 seconds. Move slowly — this is not a massage roller, it's targeted pressure.

Supportive Supplements

Supplements do not replace ergonomic corrections or exercise, but several have solid evidence for reducing muscle tension, inflammation, and pain perception. All listed below are generally safe for most adults — consult a GP or pharmacist if you are on any medications.
Supplement Why It Helps Typical Dose
Magnesium Glycinate
 Muscle relaxant. Magnesium deficiency is extremely common and directly linked to muscle tension, spasm, and poor sleep. Glycinate form is well-absorbed and gentle on the stomach. 300–400 mg before bed
Omega-3 Fish Oil
 Anti-inflammatory. Reduces systemic inflammation in muscle and connective tissue. Consistent intake over 4–6 weeks produces meaningful effect. 2–3 g EPA+DHA daily
Curcumin (Turmeric)
 Anti-inflammatory via NF-kB pathway. More effective when taken with piperine (black pepper extract) for absorption. Pairs well with omega-3. 500 mg + piperine, 2× daily
Vitamin D3 + K2
 Low vitamin D is independently associated with musculoskeletal pain and tension. Deficiency is very common in northern latitudes, especially in winter. 2,000–4,000 IU D3 daily
Collagen Peptides (Type I/III)
 Supports tendon and connective tissue repair. The levator scapulae attaches via tendons — collagen maintains elasticity and aids recovery. 10 g daily with vitamin C
Vitamin C
 Required cofactor for collagen synthesis. Also an antioxidant that reduces oxidative stress in overloaded muscle tissue. Take alongside collagen. 500–1,000 mg daily

Topical Options

🧴 Magnesium Oil Spray

Applied directly to the tight neck/shoulder area. Many find it provides meaningful local muscle relaxation. Spray 5–10 pumps, massage in gently.

🌿 Arnica Gel

Well-tolerated topical anti-inflammatory. Apply to the neck/upper shoulder junction 2–3× daily. Available at most pharmacies.

💊 Voltaren (Diclofenac Gel)

Over-the-counter NSAID gel. Reduces local inflammation without GI side effects of oral NSAIDs. Effective for 5–7 day acute flare-ups. Available at pharmacies.

⚠️

When to See a Professional

If symptoms do not improve meaningfully within 4 weeks, or if pain radiates into the arm or causes tingling/numbness in the fingers, consult a physiotherapist or GP to rule out cervical nerve involvement (radiculopathy). This protocol addresses muscle-origin pain — nerve involvement requires different management.
